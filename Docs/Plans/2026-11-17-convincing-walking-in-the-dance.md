## What the walk is made of today

The walk has four recordings for four ways (forward, back, left, right) and mixes the two nearest to the way he travels. The machinery around that mix is sound and stays:

- All recordings run on one shared step, so a foot is down in all of them at once.
- Each foot's time on the ground is lined up across the recordings, and the stride is stretched or shortened to the pace.
- A planted foot is held where it is. A change from one motion to another is eased, not snapped.
- Each recording is taken apart into what the hips, chest, arms and feet do, so levers can change one part.

What goes into the mix is the problem. Measured on the recordings themselves, at a Medium's size:

In the dance the walking time was half crouched sneak, a little under half the tall walk, and 1 to 5% the guard's own side-step. The only steps that match the stand are almost never used: under about 1 m/s my slow side-walk replaces them.

## Why it looks wrong

Five causes, in order of how much they cost. The first two are the lerp into a strange pose that you described.

**1. The mix is of unlike bodies.** Set side by side at the same moment of the step, the walk forward and the step left are 29 cm apart on average over every point of the body. Their carriages alone (how high, how wide, how turned) are 23 cm apart; the steps alone, 17 cm. A half-and-half mix of the two is a pose neither recording contains.

**2. The mix is driven by pace and direction, and the dance changes both all the time.** The way he travels swings 140 to 360 degrees a second. The leading recording changes every 0.4 to 1.1 s of walking. For 27 to 41% of the walking, two kinds of recording are each a quarter or more of the pose. My slow side-walk made it worse: it fades the crouch in and out by pace, so the hips pump with every change of speed.

**3. He never finishes a step.** Half of all spells of walking are shorter than 0.7 to 0.9 s, and a spell holds 0.5 to 1.0 steps. He sets off, changes his mind, and stops, once every 1.3 to 2.1 s.

**4. The stand and the walk are different stances.** To go from the stand to the walk forward, each foot has to move 40 cm front to back and the hips rise 9 cm. To the guard's own side-step it is 22 to 25 cm and no rise. Every stop and start crosses that gap.

**5. He walks in slow motion.** The recordings walk at 1.0 to 1.4 m/s; in the dance he goes at about 0.5 m/s. To fit, both the stride and the tempo are cut to about two thirds, and for three quarters of the walking the stride is more than 20% short of the recording's. A real fighter at that pace takes quick short steps with pauses between, not slowed ones.

One quick test supports this. With the crouched sneak taken out (the guard's own side-step slowed instead), the hips' band fell from 20 to 22 cm to 12 cm, and the 10 cm jumps from one every 5 to 8 s to one every 10 s. The 12 cm left is the tall walk against the low stand. This is one run of one fighter; the other fighter was knocked down in it, so his numbers are not comparable.

A side note, not a cause: the scene's Combat Feel has the "Prowl" try saved as everyone's movement (crouch 8 cm, lean 10°, sway and hip turn 1.6). It lowers everything by 8 cm. Rerun with the levers at rest, the band was the same 20 to 22 cm, only higher up. If that was not meant, "At rest" under "How he moves" puts it back.

## What other games do about it

The common answer is to never mix unlike poses: make the recordings alike, choose one instead of mixing, and change over only where two motions agree.

| Practice | What it says | Where we stand |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mix only like with like | Unity's own manual: "the motions that are blended must be of similar nature and timing", with foot contacts at the same points of the cycle ([Blend Trees](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/class-BlendTree.html)) | Timing: done (shared step). Nature: not. This is cause 1. |
| Turn the legs, don't mix directions | Unreal's orientation warping turns the legs to the way of travel and twists the spine back, so one forward recording covers a wide arc; it is there to "fill coverage gaps" without more recordings or mixes ([Pose Warping](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/pose-warping-in-unreal-engine)) | Not done. We mix two recordings for every diagonal. |
| Stretch the stride, but only a little | Stride warping matches stride to pace; Epic's Lyra notes advise keeping stride and play-rate changes to about 15 to 20%, and accept a little foot slide over a big stretch ([Adapting Lyra animation](https://www.unrealengine.com/tech-blog/adapting-lyra-animation-to-your-ue5-game)) | Done, but far past that at dance paces. This is cause 5. |
| Starts and stops are motions of their own | Lyra plays start and stop recordings matched to the distance left to cover, not a fade between a stand and a cycle (same article) | Not done. We fade. This is cause 4. |
| Change over where the poses agree | Motion graphs and [Snap-Together Motion](https://research.cs.wisc.edu/graphics/Gallery/kovar.vol/SnapTogetherMotion) join recordings only at shared poses. Motion matching, shown on For Honor's guarded footwork, picks the next moment by nearness of pose and of the path asked ([Clavet, GDC 2016](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1022985/Motion-Matching-and-The-Road)) | Not done. We change at any moment of the step. |
| Ease the change itself | Inertialization, from Gears of War: carry the old motion's offset and let it die away ([Bollo, GDC 2018](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025165/Inertialization-High-Performance-Animation-Transitions)). The bigger the offset, the more it shows ([Holden](https://theorangeduck.com/page/inertialization-transition-cost)) | Done. But it is asked to cover offsets of up to 80 cm. |
| Keep the body and the feet agreed | When code moves the body and recordings move the feet, they drift apart and it floats or slides; the remedies are to let the recording lead for a while, or to cap the drift ([Holden, Code vs Data Driven Displacement](https://theorangeduck.com/page/code-vs-data-driven-displacement)) | Partly. The dance's pacer moves the body; the feet follow as they can. This is cause 3. |

Full motion matching is not the plan. It needs minutes of footage shot for the purpose, all in the stance; we have thirteen short loops. Its rule, go to the nearest pose, is what steps 3 and 5 below borrow.

## The plan

Six steps, each behind its own old and new switch, each judged on the same 20 seconds of footwork. The walk's machinery stays; what changes is what it is fed and when it may change.

| Step | What changes | What you will see | How it is checked |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. The footwork course and its meter | A button in the Actions window runs the same footwork every time: advance, retreat, circle left, circle right, in and out, stop and go. Under it, the hips' band, the 10 cm jumps and the steps finished. The crouched sneak leaves the ordinary walk now. | The same test on demand, old against new, for every step below and for the dance. | The quick test above: band 20 to 22 cm down to about 12 cm. |
| 2. One carriage | Every walking recording is rebuilt on the guard's own carriage: hips' height, feet apart, left foot ahead and chest turned are the stand's. From each recording only its step is kept. | Level hips and the same stance whichever way he goes. | Band 6 cm or less. From the stand to any step: feet within 10 cm, hips level. |
| 3. Whole steps | In reach of the enemy he moves by steps. A step once begun keeps its way and length until the foot lands; a new way is taken at the landing. Standing is the end of a step, not another pose. Steps are taken at the recording's own tempo, with pauses between. | Quick short steps and stillness, in place of slow drifting. No pose change in the middle of a step. | Every spell of walking is one whole step or more. No change of recording between landings. Tempo within 20% of the recording's. |
| 4. Calmer feet in the dance | The dance asks for steps, not speeds, and keeps a decision for at least one step. It does not set off for less than a step's worth. | Fewer false starts. | Stand to walk at most once every 3 s while the other stands still. |
| 5. Turn the legs, don't mix | A diagonal is the nearest recording with the legs turned up to 45° to the way he goes, his chest and shield left facing the enemy. | No in-between poses on diagonals. | Two kinds at once: none of the walking (now 27 to 41%). |
| 6. Far and near | The tall walk and the jog are for covering ground from far off. He rises out of his stance over one step as he sets off and sinks back into it over one step as he arrives. | One clear change of carriage, made once, where it means something. | No carriage change within 2.5 m of the enemy except by a gesture. |

Balance runs through all of it. The walk already works out where his weight is against his feet each step. That goes on the meter from step 1, and it is the ground the stumbles (9c) will stand on.

The advance and the retreat are found ("What your packs hold", below): step 2 starts from an archer's walk forward and back, not from the tall walk's legs.

The kicks (9b) and the rest of the roadmap wait behind this, as you asked.
